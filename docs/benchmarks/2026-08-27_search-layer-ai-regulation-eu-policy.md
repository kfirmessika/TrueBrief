The European Union has begun enforcing key provisions of its Artificial Intelligence (AI) Act as of August 2, 2026. This marks a significant milestone in the regulation of AI within the EU.

*   **High-Risk AI Deadlines Shifted:** While some AI Act rules are now in effect, the compliance deadlines for high-risk AI systems have been extended. Standalone high-risk systems (Annex III) now have a deadline of December 2, 2027, and those embedded in regulated products (Annex I) face a deadline of August 2, 2028. This extension is due to amendments approved via the "Digital Omnibus on AI" package, which aims to provide businesses with more time to build robust governance frameworks and for harmonized technical standards to be completed.
*   **Transparency and General-Purpose AI (GPAI) Rules Active:** As of August 2, 2026, transparency obligations and rules for GPAI models are in force. This includes requirements for interactive AI systems, such as chatbots, to disclose they are AI and for AI-generated content, like deepfakes, to be clearly labeled with machine-readable marks. GPAI providers must also document technical information, implement copyright policies, and publish summaries of training data.
*   **Enforcement Powers Activated:** The European Commission's AI Office and national authorities are now actively enforcing the AI Act, including over GPAI providers. They have the power to request technical documentation, evaluate models, mandate corrective actions, and issue substantial financial penalties for non-compliance, up to €35 million or 7% of global turnover.
*   **New Prohibition on Non-Consensual Intimate Imagery:** The AI Act amendments include a new prohibition on AI systems that generate non-consensual sexually explicit content or child sexual abuse material.
*   **Limited Use of Sensitive Data for Bias Detection Now Permitted:** A new amendment allows for a regulated exemption enabling the processing of "special category data" (e.g., race, gender) strictly for the purpose of detecting and correcting bias in AI models, addressing a previous paradox in AI development.
*   **Extraterritorial Reach:** The AI Act's regulations apply to AI systems placed on the EU market or whose outputs are used within the EU, regardless of where the provider or deployer is located. This means American AI companies, for example, can fall under European regulatory oversight.
